CareerMatch — PatriotHacks Hackathon

PatriotHacks 2023 · 2024 · George Mason University · 24-hour build

The brief

Two PatriotHacks runs, both 24-hour builds. The premise was simple: undergraduates pick majors without great visibility into where they actually lead. Could a small web app take a student's declared major and elective pattern and surface career tracks they hadn't considered?

What we built

A four-person team, three layers:

  • Frontend in plain HTML/CSS/JS. A short questionnaire (major, electives so far, interests). No framework, because we wanted to ship before the judging cutoff.
  • Analytics surface in Tableau. We loaded a small static dataset of major-to-career mappings (sourced from Bureau of Labor Statistics career outlooks plus GMU's own program pages) and used Tableau to visualize the matches and let users explore them after the questionnaire.
  • Hosting on AWS — S3 for the static frontend, embedded Tableau Public for the dashboards. Cheap, fast to stand up, no server to manage during demo hour.

What I learned

Two things, both about scope.

First: cut "nice-to-haves" early. We had a list of features at hour zero that included a recommendation engine and login persistence. By hour eight we'd cut both, and the demo was strictly better for it. The recommendation engine in particular would have eaten the night and added very little to the judging story.

Second: judging is its own design problem. The judges walk past your table for 90 seconds. The questionnaire-to-dashboard handoff has to be visually obvious from across the room. We re-did the landing screen at hour 18 just to make the demo arc cleaner.

Why this is on a cybersecurity portfolio

Hackathons are about shipping under pressure with incomplete information, which is half the actual job in security operations and incident response. PatriotHacks taught me to scope, cut, and ship better than any class did. The other half was getting more comfortable with AWS — which has carried into the data-center work I do now.
