Off the script for a second: dancing with Kappa Phi Gamma

2026-04-05 · 3 min read · life · kphig · gmu

The technical posts can wait. Capstone's almost shipped, finals are around the corner, and somewhere in the middle of all that, Showcase happened and I haven't said a word about it on here.

I'm a sister of Kappa Phi Gamma at George Mason. We're the South Asian-interest sorority, founded back in 1998 (at UT Austin, of all places, and only later working our way out here). The Mason chapter is small but very loud, and twice a year we put something together and get on a stage.

If you don't know what we do — we dance fusion. Bollywood, bhangra, classical Indian, hip-hop, contemporary if a song calls for it. Every year somebody pitches a song nobody's heard before. Every year somebody else figures out how to map it to eight counts of choreo. By the time show night arrives it looks like the whole arrangement was always meant to go together, which is a small kind of magic.

Showcase is exhausting in the way capstone is exhausting. Late practices that end with everyone sitting on the floor of a studio at midnight. A small group of people you fully rely on. One big public output at the end. Same shape, completely different energy. I think that's part of why both have mattered to my time at Mason. Cybersecurity coursework teaches me how to be careful and skeptical. Showcase teaches me how to commit to a thing and not flinch.

To my sisters: love you. To the alumni who came back this year to watch — it really did mean something. And to anybody reading this because you're considering rushing Kappa Phi Gamma: do it.

— Nandini
